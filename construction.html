Concrete Volume Calculator

Use this concrete calculator to estimate the volume of concrete needed for a rectangular slab in cubic feet and cubic yards, plus the number of 60 lb or 80 lb pre-mixed bags. Enter the length, width, and thickness of the slab. Add 5–10% for waste, spillage, and uneven subgrade when ordering.

Worked example

10 ft × 10 ft slab at 4 inches thick:

  • Volume: 33.33 cu ft (1.23 cu yd)
  • 60 lb bags: 75
  • 80 lb bags: 56

With 10% waste, order about 37 cu ft or 83 × 60 lb bags.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Assumes a rectangular slab of uniform thickness
  • Bag yields: 60 lb ≈ 0.45 cu ft; 80 lb ≈ 0.60 cu ft
  • Does not include waste, spillage, or over-excavation — add 5–10%
  • Does not include reinforcement, footings, or formwork

How the calculation works

Volume (cu ft) = Length (ft) × Width (ft) × Thickness (ft)

Volume (cu yd) = Volume (cu ft) ÷ 27

Bags = Volume (cu ft) ÷ bag yield (0.45 or 0.60 cu ft)

Thickness in inches is divided by 12 to convert to feet.

Frequently asked questions

How much extra concrete should I order?

Add 5–10% for typical slab work. Uneven subgrade, deep spots, or forms that bow can require more. Running short mid-pour is far more costly than a small overage.

How many 80 lb bags are in a cubic yard?

About 45 bags of 80 lb concrete per cubic yard (27 ÷ 0.60). For 60 lb bags, about 60 per cubic yard.

Does this work for footings or columns?

No. This calculator is for rectangular slabs. Footings and columns need their own volume calculations based on their cross-section and depth.
