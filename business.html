Profit Margin Calculator

Use this profit margin calculator to find your gross profit, profit margin percentage, and markup percentage from a product's cost and selling price. Profit margin tells you what share of each sale is profit; markup tells you how much you added to cost. Both are useful for pricing decisions, but they measure different things.

Worked example

Cost $40, selling price $100:

  • Profit: $60
  • Profit margin: 60%
  • Markup: 150%

Note how margin (60%) and markup (150%) differ for the same sale — they use different bases.

Assumptions and limitations

  • "Cost" means the direct cost of the item (COGS); does not include overhead, shipping, or fees
  • Margin is calculated on revenue; markup is calculated on cost
  • Results are pre-tax and do not account for discounts or returns

How the calculation works

Profit = Selling price − Cost

Profit margin = (Profit ÷ Selling price) × 100

Markup = (Profit ÷ Cost) × 100

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin is profit as a percentage of the selling price. Markup is profit as a percentage of cost. A 50% margin corresponds to a 100% markup. Confusing the two is a common pricing error.

What is a good profit margin?

It varies by industry. Retail often runs 2–10%, software 70–90%, and services 20–40%. Compare your margin to your industry benchmark rather than a universal target.

Should I price based on margin or markup?

Decide on a target margin first (e.g., 40%), then set price = cost ÷ (1 − margin). Markup is useful when you start from cost and want to see what price results.
