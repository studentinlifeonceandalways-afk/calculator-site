Rental Yield Calculator

Use this rental yield calculator to estimate the gross and net rental yield of an investment property. Gross yield measures annual rent as a percentage of property value; net yield subtracts operating expenses first. Both help you compare properties, but net yield is the more realistic measure of return before financing costs.

Worked example

$350,000 property, $24,000 annual rent, $4,000 annual expenses:

  • Gross yield: 6.86%
  • Net yield: 5.71%
  • Annual net income: $20,000

Assumptions and limitations

  • Property value is the purchase price or current market value, not the loan amount
  • Expenses include taxes, insurance, maintenance, management, and vacancy — not mortgage payments
  • Does not account for capital appreciation, tax benefits, or financing costs
  • Assumes full-year occupancy unless vacancy is included in expenses

How the calculation works

Gross yield = (Annual rent ÷ Property value) × 100

Net yield = ((Annual rent − Annual expenses) ÷ Property value) × 100

Frequently asked questions

What is a good rental yield?

It depends on the market. In many US markets, gross yields of 5–8% are considered reasonable for residential rentals; high-cost cities often run lower. Net yield is the better comparison metric across properties.

Should mortgage payments be included in expenses?

No. Yield measures the property's return independent of how it is financed. Financing costs affect cash flow, not yield. Use a separate cash-flow calculator for mortgage-adjusted returns.

Gross vs net yield — which should I use?

Use gross yield for quick screening and net yield for serious comparison. Net yield accounts for the real cost of operating the property and is closer to actual return.
