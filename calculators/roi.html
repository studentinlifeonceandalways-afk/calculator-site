ROI Calculator

Use this ROI calculator to find the return on an investment from its initial cost, its final value, and (optionally) how long you held it. ROI shows total return as a percentage of the initial cost; annualized ROI converts that to a per-year rate so you can compare investments with different holding periods. This is the simple return, not the time-weighted IRR.

Worked example

$1,000 cost, $1,500 final value after 2 years:

  • Net gain: $500
  • Total ROI: 50%
  • Annualized ROI: 22.47%

Annualized ROI uses the compound growth rate, not simple division, so it compares fairly to other annual returns.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Does not include taxes, fees, or transaction costs — subtract from final value for net ROI
  • Assumes no interim cash flows (dividends, rent, distributions)
  • Annualized ROI is the CAGR (compound annual growth rate), not IRR
  • Requires holding period > 0 for annualized result; otherwise only total ROI is shown

How the calculation works

Net gain = Final value − Initial cost

Total ROI (%) = (Net gain ÷ Initial cost) × 100

Annualized ROI (%) = ((Final ÷ Initial)1/years − 1) × 100

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between ROI and annualized ROI?

ROI is total return over the holding period. Annualized ROI converts that to a per-year rate, so a 50% return over 2 years (22.47% annualized) can be compared to a 30% return over 5 years (5.39% annualized).

How is this different from IRR?

IRR (internal rate of return) accounts for the timing of cash flows (dividends, partial sales, additional investments). Simple ROI assumes a single initial outflow and one final inflow.

Should ROI be calculated before or after tax?

Both are useful. Pre-tax ROI compares investment performance; after-tax ROI (net ROI) is what you actually keep. Use after-tax figures for personal decisions.
