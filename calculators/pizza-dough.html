Pizza Dough Calculator

Use this pizza dough calculator to get exact flour, water, yeast, and salt amounts based on baker's percentages. Enter the number of pizzas, the weight of each dough ball, and the hydration percentage. Baker's percentage expresses every ingredient as a percentage of total flour weight — 60% hydration means 60 g water for every 100 g flour. Standard Neapolitan-style dough uses 60–65% hydration, 2.5–3% salt, and 0.2–0.5% yeast for a cold ferment.

Worked example

2 pizzas, 250 g per ball, 62% hydration, 2.5% salt, 0.4% yeast:

  • Total dough: 500 g
  • Flour: 302 g
  • Water: 187 g
  • Salt: 7.5 g
  • Yeast: 1.2 g

Assumptions and limitations

  • Baker's percentage: flour = 100%, all other ingredients expressed as % of flour weight
  • Dough ball weight includes flour, water, salt, and yeast — not toppings
  • Yeast percentage assumes instant dry yeast for a 24–72 hour cold ferment
  • For same-day dough, use 1–2% yeast instead

How the calculation works

Total dough = Pizzas × Ball weight

Flour = Total dough ÷ (1 + hydration/100 + salt/100 + yeast/100)

Water = Flour × hydration/100

Salt = Flour × salt/100

Yeast = Flour × yeast/100

Frequently asked questions

What hydration should I use?

60–65% for standard Neapolitan or New York style. 70–75% for a lighter, airier neo-Neapolitan crust (harder to handle). 55–60% for cracker-thin or pan pizza. Higher hydration produces a more open crumb.

How much yeast for a cold ferment?

0.2–0.5% instant dry yeast for 24–72 hours in the fridge. Same-day dough: 1–2%. The longer the ferment, the less yeast you need.

How big should each dough ball be?

For a 12-inch pizza: 250–280 g. For a 14-inch: 320–360 g. For 10-inch: 180–220 g. Smaller balls for Neapolitan (200–250 g for 10–12 inch), larger for New York (350 g for 16-inch).

What flour should I use?

"00" flour for Neapolitan; bread flour (12–13% protein) for New York style; all-purpose works for a softer, more home-style crust.
