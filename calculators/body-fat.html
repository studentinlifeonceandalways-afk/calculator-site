Body Fat Calculator

Use this body fat calculator to estimate body fat percentage using the U.S. Navy circumference method. It uses height, neck, and waist measurements (plus hip circumference for women) to estimate body fat without calipers or a DEXA scan. Accuracy is typically within ±3–4 percentage points compared with clinical methods, making it useful for tracking trends over time rather than one-off measurement.

Worked example

Male, 175 cm, neck 38 cm, waist 85 cm:

  • Estimated body fat: ~17%
  • Category: Fitness

Body fat categories vary by sex and age; the result should be read as a trend indicator.

Body fat categories (approximate)

Men:

  • Essential: 2–5%
  • Athletic: 6–13%
  • Fitness: 14–17%
  • Average: 18–24%
  • Obese: 25%+

Women:

  • Essential: 10–13%
  • Athletic: 14–20%
  • Fitness: 21–24%
  • Average: 25–31%
  • Obese: 32%+

Assumptions and limitations

  • Uses the U.S. Navy circumference formula; accuracy is ±3–4 percentage points
  • Measurements must be taken at correct anatomical locations (neck at narrowest, waist at navel)
  • Less accurate for very muscular or very obese individuals
  • Not a clinical measurement; DEXA or hydrostatic weighing are more accurate

How the calculation works

Men: %BF = 495 ÷ (1.0324 − 0.19077×log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456×log10(height)) − 450

Women: %BF = 495 ÷ (1.29579 − 0.35004×log10(waist + hip − neck) + 0.22100×log10(height)) − 450

All measurements in centimeters.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is the Navy method?

Typically within ±3–4% of DEXA. It's better for tracking changes over time than for absolute measurement. Take measurements consistently — same time of day, same tape tension.

Where exactly should I measure?

Neck: narrowest point below the larynx. Waist: at the navel for men, at the narrowest point for women. Hip: widest point of the buttocks. Keep the tape level and snug but not compressing the skin.

Why do men and women have different formulas?

Men and women store fat differently (women carry more subcutaneous fat, particularly around hips and thighs), so the circumference-based regression is sex-specific.
