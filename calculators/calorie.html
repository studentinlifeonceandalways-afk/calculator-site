Calorie (TDEE) Calculator

Use this TDEE calculator to estimate your Total Daily Energy Expenditure — the calories your body burns per day at your activity level. It uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate basal metabolic rate (BMR), then multiplies by an activity factor. TDEE is the calorie intake that would keep your weight stable; eat below it to lose weight, above it to gain. Individual metabolism varies, so treat the result as a starting point.

Worked example

30-year-old male, 70 kg, 175 cm, moderately active:

  • BMR: 1,649 kcal/day
  • TDEE: 2,556 kcal/day
  • Mild weight loss (−15%): ~2,173 kcal/day
  • Mild weight gain (+15%): ~2,939 kcal/day

Assumptions and limitations

  • Mifflin-St Jeor equation; not valid during pregnancy
  • Activity multiplier is a self-reported average
  • Not a substitute for individual clinical assessment

How the calculation works

Male BMR = 10×weight + 6.25×height − 5×age + 5

Female BMR = 10×weight + 6.25×height − 5×age − 161

TDEE = BMR × activity factor (1.2 to 1.9)

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR is calories burned at complete rest. TDEE includes BMR plus all activity. TDEE is the number you eat against for weight management.

How accurate is this calculator?

Mifflin-St Jeor is typically accurate within ±10% for most people. Track weight for 2–3 weeks and adjust intake by 100–200 calories based on actual results.

How many calories should I cut to lose weight?

A common starting point is 15–20% below TDEE, which typically produces 0.5–1 kg per week of loss.
