Cap Rate Calculator

Use this cap rate calculator to compute the capitalization rate of an income property from its net operating income (NOI) and current market value (or purchase price). Cap rate is a quick way to compare the yield of different properties independent of financing. Higher cap rate usually means higher return but higher risk; lower cap rate often means a more stable, lower-risk asset in a strong market.

Worked example

NOI $24,000, property value $400,000:

  • Cap rate: 6.00%

At the same NOI, a $500,000 purchase price would produce a 4.80% cap rate.

Assumptions and limitations

  • NOI = gross rent − operating expenses (excludes mortgage, depreciation, and income tax)
  • Uses current value or purchase price, not loan amount
  • Does not account for capital expenditures, vacancy beyond what's in NOI, or appreciation
  • Cap rate is a snapshot; forward cap rate uses projected NOI and may differ

How the calculation works

Cap rate (%) = (NOI ÷ Property value) × 100

To compute NOI: gross rental income minus vacancy, property tax, insurance, maintenance, management, and other operating costs. Exclude mortgage payments.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good cap rate?

It varies by market and asset class. Residential rentals often run 4–8%; commercial properties 6–12%. Higher cap rates mean higher yield but often higher risk or slower-growth markets. Compare to local comparables, not a single universal number.

How is cap rate different from rental yield?

Rental yield typically starts from gross rent; cap rate uses net operating income after expenses. Cap rate is the standard metric for commercial property; gross yield is more common for residential rentals.

Should mortgage payments be in NOI?

No. NOI is calculated before financing so cap rate is a property-level metric, independent of how it's financed. Mortgage payments affect cash flow, not cap rate.
